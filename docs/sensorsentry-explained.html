PS 18 · Explained simply

Someone can lie to a drone about where it is

And the drone will believe it completely. This page explains the problem, the attack, and what we are building — in plain language, with no technical background needed.

The problem

GPS is a whisper, and anyone can shout over it

A drone knows where it is by listening to satellites in space. Those satellites are extremely far away, so their signal arrives very weak — like someone whispering to you from across a field.

A person on the ground with a cheap radio box can simply shout louder than the whisper, pretending to be the satellite. The drone listens to the louder, clearer voice — and now believes a completely fake location.

Think of it this way

Someone swapped all the road signs. You follow every sign perfectly — and still end up in the wrong city.

You did not make a mistake. You trusted the sign, and the sign lied. That is exactly what happens to the drone. And the worst part: it has no idea. It is completely confident, and completely wrong.

A real situation

A hospital sends medicine by drone to a village 12 km away

On a normal day: it takes off, follows GPS, delivers, comes back. Here is what happens on the day someone attacks it.

1

The attacker switches on his box

He parks somewhere near the flight route. No special skill needed — the equipment is cheap and easy to get now.

2

The lie starts small

The fake location is only a few metres off at first. Nothing looks wrong. No alarm anywhere. This is deliberate — a small lie is not noticed.

3

The drone corrects itself — in the wrong direction

The drone thinks it has drifted off route, so it steers to fix it. But because the position is fake, "correcting" actually pushes it further away from the real route. The drone is now flying itself off course, and doing it carefully.

4

Five minutes later it is 2 km away

It is far from where it believes it is. It still reports everything as normal, because as far as it knows, everything is normal.

5

It lands in an empty field

The attacker walks over and picks it up. The medicine is gone. The drone is gone — lakhs of rupees.

6

Nobody can even prove what happened

The flight log recorded the fake positions too. So the record shows a normal flight. No evidence for the company, and none for the insurance claim.

Our idea

The drone has other senses. We just ask them.

GPS is not the drone's only way of knowing things about itself. It already carries three more sensors — and nobody is using them to check whether GPS is telling the truth.

Sense 1

An inner ear

Feels speed, turning and tilting. Like walking across a dark room with your eyes closed — you still roughly know where you have reached.

Sense 2

A pressure sensor

Measures air pressure, which tells it how high it is. Completely separate from GPS, and it does not listen to any radio.

Sense 3

A compass

Knows which direction the drone is facing, using the Earth's magnetic field.

The one question our software keeps asking

GPS says "you are here." Do the other senses agree?

Normally they all agree. But when someone fakes the GPS, the GPS starts describing movements the drone never actually felt. GPS claims "you just moved 50 metres east" — but the inner ear felt no eastward movement at all. That disagreement is the proof.

And this is the part that makes it work: an attacker can fake a radio signal coming from far away. He cannot fake what the drone physically feels inside its own body. To beat us, he would have to fool every sensor at once, in a way that stays perfectly consistent — which is a far harder problem than shouting over a whisper.

How our software runs

Seven steps, repeating every second

1

Listen

Read all the sensors continuously — GPS, inner ear, pressure, compass.

2

Predict

Using only the inner ear, work out separately: "based on what I actually felt, where should I be right now?"

This is a second opinion that owes nothing to GPS.

3

Compare

Measure the gap between the place GPS claims, and the place the drone's own body says.

4

Judge

A small gap is completely normal — no sensor is perfect. But a gap that keeps growing steadily in one direction is not noise. That is someone pulling.

5

Score

Give GPS a trust score that moves gradually: 100% → 70% → 20%. Not a simple yes or no.

A yes/no alarm that fires wrongly mid-flight is as dangerous as the attack itself.

6

React

When trust falls too low, stop believing GPS. Navigate using the inner ear instead, hold the correct route, and alert the operator that the drone is under attack.

7

Record

Save the proof of what happened — when it started, how it was detected, what the real path was. For the company, and for the insurance claim.

Why this is new

Everyone else solves it by selling you hardware

Today, the real protection against this attack is a special antenna fitted to each drone. It works — and almost nobody has it.

Because it means opening up every single drone, fitting a new part, and paying for that part on every unit. So in practice, the lakhs of drones already flying today stay completely unprotected. The hardware answer protects the fleet you buy next year.

Ours is only software. You install it into drones that are already flying. Nothing to buy, nothing to open, nothing to fit. It works using the sensors the drone already has.

That single difference is the whole business. It is why a company can exist around this, and it is the first sentence to say to a judge.

For the hackathon

What we actually build in two days

No drone. No hardware. Everything runs on one laptop — which is the point, not a shortcut.

Piece 1

A pretend drone

A program that flies an imaginary route and produces the sensor readings a real drone would produce. We also use it to launch the attack, whenever we want.

Piece 2

The detector

The actual product. It watches those readings and works out whether the GPS is lying — the seven steps above.

Piece 3

The screen

A live map showing two paths — where GPS claims the drone is, and where it really is — with the trust score falling as the attack proceeds.
