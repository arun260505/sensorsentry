GPS spoofing

An attacker fakes the satellite signal. The vehicle believes a completely false position.

False readings

No attacker at all — a sensor is failing, and reports wrong numbers with total confidence.

Manipulated inputs

The attacker changes the physical world at the sensor — a magnet by the compass.

Three problems, not one. Most teams answer only the first. All three look identical at first, and they demand opposite responses — so we answer all three.

A spoofing walk-off, as it happens. The vehicle's real track, the position GPS claims, and the gap between them that gives the attack away.

02Proposed solution

The vehicle has other senses. Nobody is asking them.

Our answer, in one line

An attacker can fake the signal a vehicle receives. He cannot fake what the vehicle physically feels.

1

Build a second opinion

From its own motion, heading and wheels, the vehicle works out where it must be — without ever reading GPS.

2

Find who disagrees

Every sensor is checked against every other. If one disagrees with all the rest while they agree with each other, that one is lying.

3

Attack, or breakdown?

A fault is random and messy. An attack is smooth, and pulls steadily one way. Drop the liar, keep navigating.

The one question it keeps asking

GPS says "you are here." Do the other senses agree? When they don't, that disagreement is the proof.

03The sensors · every one, and what it is for

Six independent witnesses, all already fitted

Not one of these is new hardware. Every drone and every tracked truck already carries them — which is why this ships as a software update rather than a workshop visit.

GNSS
Global Navigation Satellite System

The satellite receiver — the one being lied to.

On the vehicle: antenna on the roof or airframe
an attacker can fake this
IMU · accelerometer
Inertial Measurement Unit

Feels every push, brake and bump — the vehicle's inner ear. Add it up over time and you know where you are.

On the vehicle: a chip inside the tracker box
out of his reach
IMU · gyroscope
Inertial Measurement Unit

The same chip, measuring spin. It knows the moment the vehicle actually turns.

On the vehicle: the same chip as the accelerometer
out of his reach
BARO
Barometer — pressure sensor

Air thins as you climb, so pressure gives height — the way an aircraft altimeter always has.

On the vehicle: a vented port on the same board
only by physical means
MAG
Magnetometer — digital compass

Reads the Earth's magnetic field to know which way the vehicle points. A compass with no moving parts.

On the vehicle: away from the motors, often on a mast
only by physical means
ODOM
Wheel odometry

Counts wheel turns to measure real distance covered. Trucks only — a drone has no wheels.

On the vehicle: the ABS sensor already at the wheel
out of his reach

The point of the last row: GPS is the only one an attacker can touch from a distance. The other five, he would have to reach the vehicle itself — which is the whole reason this works.

04Naming the liar, and knowing why

A diagnosis, not an alarm

Cause 1 · a sensor fault

Random, jumpy, incoherent. The error points in no particular direction, and the sensor usually fails its own health check too. → Drop it, keep operating, flag the vehicle for service.

Cause 2 · a spoofing attack

Smooth, steady, purposeful — pulling consistently one way. Too clean to be a fault: real hardware fails messily, an attacker wants a specific destination. → Alarm, switch navigation, preserve evidence, check the fleet.

Cause 3 · manipulated environment

One physical measurement shifts while everything else stays honest — the compass swings but the gyro felt no turn. → Ignore that sensor, warn that interference is present nearby.

And when we are not sure

Below the confidence threshold we return unclassified and default to the safe response. The system is allowed to say it does not know.

06Novelty

Four things that make this ours

The vulnerability is well known and the physics are public. The novelty is not in noticing that GPS can be faked — it is in where the defence sits and what it does once it fires.

01

No hardware at all

Today's real protection is an anti-spoofing antenna fitted per vehicle. It works, and almost nobody has it, because it means opening every unit and paying per unit. The hardware answer protects the fleet you buy next year. Ours protects the fleet running today.

02

It names the guilty sensor

Not "something is wrong" — "the compass is lying, and the other four agree that it is." An alarm with no subject leaves a human guessing during the worst possible minute. That difference decides whether the operator can act.

03

Attack or fault — the question everyone skips

Others stop at "anomaly detected." We answer why, because a broken sensor and a deliberate attack demand opposite responses. It is also the only way one system covers all three problems PS 18 names.

04

The fleet checks itself

One vehicle reporting a GPS problem might be a fault. Four in the same area at the same moment is certainly an attack — and you now know roughly where the attacker is standing. Detection gets stronger as the fleet grows.

Claim 04 is also the business. Every new customer makes the product better for every existing customer, because each vehicle added is another witness on the map. A competitor can copy our code in a month. They cannot copy the fleet.

07What we can expect

What the finished system will do — and where it fails

1

Spoofing caught, GPS namedA walk-off attack is detected, gnss is named as the liar, and the vehicle stays on its true route afterwards.

2

Zero false alarmsA clean run with hard turns and braking finishes with no alerts. This is a gate we check after every change.

3

A broken sensor is called a faultNot an attack. Different symptom, different response, correct diagnosis.

4

A magnet is called interferenceThe compass swings, the gyro felt no turn, and the system says so.

5

Two liars → "I don't know"Spoof GPS and the compass at once and it returns cannot_isolate rather than a confident wrong answer.

6

Four vehicles → one attack zoneAttacked together they produce one event and one circle on the map. One vehicle alone produces no circle.

7

Trucks by configuration onlySwitching from drone to truck changes settings, not code.

8

Every incident replays exactlyThe stored record reproduces an identical verdict, which is what an insurer or an investigator actually needs.

The honest limit

A very slow spoofer — under roughly 0.2 m/s — moves the vehicle no faster than our own dead-reckoning drift, and hides inside it. We will say this on stage rather than wait to be asked.
